---

## Who this is for

- High school and undergraduate students curious about astronomy  
- Anyone who wants to use **Python for science** without becoming a full-time programmer  
- Learners who prefer **doing** (plots, data, classification) over pure memorization  

**You do not need advanced physics or deep coding experience.**  
We start from the ground up. Curiosity is enough.

---

## What you will learn

By the end of this course you will be able to:

- Explain how stars are classified and why  
- Interpret a Hertzsprung–Russell (H–R) diagram  
- Understand what stellar spectra reveal about temperature and composition  
- Plot and explore astronomy-style data with Python  
- Think in research-style workflows, not only textbook definitions  

---

## Learning path

Complete the modules **in order**. Each one builds on the last.

1. [**Module 1 — Foundations & data**]({{ site.baseurl }}/module-1/) — Stars, data thinking, and your first HR ideas with Python context  
2. [**Module 2 — Star classification & H–R theory**]({{ site.baseurl }}/module-2/) — Temperature, luminosity, and the structure of the HR diagram  
3. [**Module 3 — Spectroscopy**]({{ site.baseurl }}/module-3/) — How light encodes composition, temperature, and motion  
4. [**Lab materials — Code, data & video**]({{ site.baseurl }}/lab/) — Colab notebook, dataset, and walkthrough video  

---

## Progress tip

Treat each module like a lab session: read, pause, try the ideas, then move on.  
At the halfway point (after Module 2), use the **check-in** on that page so you lock in what you learned before continuing.

When you finish the lab, complete the [graduation form](https://tally.so/r/PdVor1) to request your certificate.
